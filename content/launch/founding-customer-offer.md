A done-with-you CLEAR report, run personally by Erik for the first five customers.
This is the fastest credible path to the launch goal: *someone buys a ~€200 report and
is genuinely happy about it.* It de-risks every worry at once — Erik controls quality by
hand, volume is capped at five, and the price is defended by a happiness guarantee.

**How this differs from the self-serve product.** The self-serve app sells a €99 Report
Pass that a customer runs themselves. The Founding Partner Report is a separate, premium,
*done-with-you* engagement: Erik takes the customer's challenge through CLEAR himself, curates
the output with his own expertise, and delivers a polished report plus two calls. Same engine,
expert in the loop, human accountability. Sold via a dedicated Stripe Payment Link, not the
in-app checkout.

## 2. Positioning notes (internal — why this works)

- **It sells the human, not the software.** At launch the credible thing to sell is Erik's
  judgment, delivered faster by CLEAR. The app is the engine; the psychologist is the product.
- **€200 is a considered-but-easy B2B number.** Below any procurement threshold, above
  "cheap enough to ignore." It sits at ~2x the self-serve €99 Report Pass, which is exactly
  what a done-with-you version with two live calls should cost.
- **Scarcity is real, not manufactured.** Five is a genuine ceiling on Erik's time at ≤4h per
  sale (see §4). Do not inflate it. When the five are gone, the honest next step is a waitlist
  or the self-serve product.
- **The guarantee is the price-conviction tool.** It moves the risk from the buyer to Erik.
  Erik's real exposure is small (his time), and it converts the "is it worth €200?" objection
  into "worst case, I get my money back." Honor it instantly — a fast refund buys more goodwill
  than a saved €200.
- **The testimonial is the compounding asset.** Five happy partners become the proof the
  self-serve launch is missing today (zero testimonials, zero named customers). This is how you
  buy that inventory honestly.

## 3. Qualification — who this is for (and who it is not)

Use this on the intake call to protect both parties. A bad-fit sale is the thing most likely to
trigger a refund and a lukewarm testimonial, so it is worth disqualifying early.

**A good fit has all of these:**

- A **specific behavior** they want a specific **group** to do more or less of — customers,
  employees, citizens, patients, users. ("We want more of our new users to complete setup in
  week one." "We want managers to actually run their check-ins.")
- A **named target group** they can describe, not "the whole market."
- **Some material to share** — a brief, existing data, a strategy doc, notes. CLEAR is far
  stronger with real intake than with a blank prompt.
- **Authority or influence** to act on the report. A partner who cannot do anything with the
  findings will not be happy, whatever the quality.
- Comfort with a **first-cohort, expert-run** product where the deliverable is analysis and a
  plan, not a done implementation.

**Not a fit (say so kindly and redirect):**

- Wants **strategy consulting**, org design, or a change program *delivered* — that is Erik's
  consulting engagement, not a €200 report. Route to a consulting call.
- Has **no defined behavior or group** yet ("we just need help figuring out our direction").
  Offer a paid discovery conversation instead, or the free teaser to sharpen the question.
- Wants CLEAR to **make a decision for them** or produce a guaranteed outcome. CLEAR produces a
  diagnosis and a plan; behavior change is theirs to run.
- Needs to upload **special-category or heavily regulated personal data** (health records,
  named individuals' sensitive data) before the data-processing terms are finalized. Defer.
- Is really asking for a **discounted retainer**. The founding price is for the report, once.

**Disqualify script:** *"Honestly, I don't think the Founding Partner Report is the right thing
for you here — it's built to move one specific behavior, and what you're describing is closer to
[strategy work / an open exploration]. I'd rather point you to the right thing than sell you the
wrong one. Can I suggest [alternative]?"* Disqualifying well is how you keep the five slots for
buyers who will be delighted.

## 4. Fulfillment checklist — Erik's steps per sale

**Time budget: ≤ 4 hours per partner.** If a sale is trending over budget, it is usually a
scope problem (a bad-fit challenge) — name it and re-scope on the debrief, don't absorb it
silently.

**Tools:** the CLEAR app (`clear-framework.com/app`, logged in with AI mode `live`), the intake
call link (Outlook Book-with-me, already in `src/config/site.ts`), email (`erik@eb-consulting.se`),
